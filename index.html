App Store

Seven published apps

It started in 2022 with a program that read my phone's sensors and wrote out the words I drew in the air. That experiment became a family of apps where motion, the camera, or a graph — never a button — is the control surface.

  • AntColony Graph Theory

    Games

    Ants walk a graph, drop pheromone, and the trails evaporate. With no central control, the colony converges on the shortest path to food — emergence you can watch and reshape live.

    App Store

  • PenDraw

    Graphics & Design

    Turns the phone itself into a pen. Motion sensors track how you move the device and render the stroke in real time on a clean, distraction-free canvas.

    App Store

  • PenFinger

    Entertainment

    Draws with your fingertip in mid-air. The camera tracks your hand and converts real-world movement into 2D strokes — processed entirely on device.

    App Store

  • PenDraw Maze

    Entertainment

    The pen, turned into a game. Steer a continuous line through a maze using nothing but the tilt of your hand — no buttons, no touch controls.

    App Store

  • AirViolin

    Music

    Hold the phone like a bow. Pitch angle selects the note, bowing speed sets the volume, and stopping your arm stops the sound instantly.

    App Store

  • AirDrums Epic

    Music

    Four drum zones mapped to rotation, struck by swinging your arm down. Hit harder and it plays louder — velocity-sensitive audio from raw sensor data.

    App Store

  • PenTimer

    Productivity

    A 25/5 focus timer that writes every finished session into Apple Calendar, so your real focused hours show up as blocks on a daily timeline.

    App Store

All seven are free, with no accounts and no data collection. See the full developer page →

About

Background

I'm a Computer Engineering and Industrial Engineering double major at Boğaziçi University, graduating in January 2027. The combination is deliberate: computer engineering gives me the models and the code, industrial engineering gives me the optimization and the systems thinking. Most of what I enjoy sits where those two meet — routing problems, forecasting pipelines, and interfaces that read the physical world.
